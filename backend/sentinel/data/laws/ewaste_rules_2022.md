# E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022

## Rule 3 — Applicability
These rules apply to every producer, manufacturer, dealer, refurbisher, dismantler, and recycler of electrical and electronic equipment (EEE) listed in Schedule I, as well as bulk consumers and collection centres. Consumer-level generators of e-waste are also covered as waste generators.

## Rule 5 — Channelisation to Authorised Recyclers
E-waste must be channelised only to authorised dismantlers or recyclers. Producers must establish a collection mechanism or join a Producer Responsibility Organisation (PRO) to ensure e-waste does not enter the informal sector. Bulk consumers (government offices, companies, institutions) must hand over e-waste exclusively to authorised agencies.

## Rule 6 — Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)
Producers are required to meet annual EPR targets expressed as a percentage of EEE placed on the market in prior years. They must register on the EPR portal maintained by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and submit annual returns. Failure to meet EPR targets invites environmental compensation.

## Rule 15 — Prohibition on Informal Handling and Open Dumping
No person shall dismantle, recycle, or dispose of e-waste in any manner other than through authorised facilities. Open dumping of e-waste on roads, vacant land, drains, or public places is prohibited. Informal workers using acid stripping or open burning to recover metals are in violation.

## Rule 21 — Enforcement Authority
The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) is the nodal enforcement authority at the national level. The Karnataka State Pollution Control Board (KSPCB) enforces compliance within Karnataka, has powers to inspect, issue directions, and initiate prosecution under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. Urban Local Bodies (e.g., BBMP) are responsible for ensuring e-waste is not mixed with municipal solid waste.

## Rule 22 — Penalties
Contraventions are punishable under Section 15 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, with imprisonment up to five years, a fine up to one lakh rupees, or both. Continuing violations attract additional fines for each day of contravention.
