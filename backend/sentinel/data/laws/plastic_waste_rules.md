# Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 (amended 2022)

## Rule 4 — Prohibition on Single-Use Plastics
With effect from 1 July 2022, the manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale, and use of identified single-use plastic (SUP) items is prohibited. Banned items include plastic sticks for ear buds, balloons, flags, plates, cups, glasses, cutlery (forks, spoons, knives), straw, trays, wrapping or packing films around sweet boxes, invitation cards, cigarette packets, PVC banners less than 100 microns, and stirrers.

## Rule 4A — Minimum Thickness
Plastic carry bags must have a minimum thickness of 75 microns (increased to 120 microns from 31 December 2022). Thinner bags are banned from manufacture and sale.

## Rule 5 — Prohibition on Open Burning of Plastic Waste
Open burning of plastic waste at any location — including waste dump sites, roadsides, or premises — is strictly prohibited. Open burning releases toxic dioxins and furans and violates both these rules and the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981.

## Rule 6 — Extended Producer Responsibility for Plastic Packaging
Producers, importers, and brand owners using plastic packaging must register on the CPCB EPR portal and meet annual collection and recycling targets. Plastic packaging waste must be channelised to registered recyclers.

## Rule 13 — Enforcement Authority
The State Pollution Control Board (KSPCB in Karnataka) enforces compliance for producers, importers, and brand owners. Urban Local Bodies (BBMP in Bengaluru) enforce the ban on use and sale of prohibited items at the retail and street level. CPCB oversees the EPR registry and national compliance.

## Rule 15 — Penalties
Violations are punishable under Section 15 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. State PCBs may also direct closure or suspension of operations. ULBs may levy spot fines under their respective municipal acts.
