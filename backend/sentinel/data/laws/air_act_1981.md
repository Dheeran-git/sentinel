# Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

## Section 2 — Definitions: Air Pollutant
An "air pollutant" is any solid, liquid, or gaseous substance present in the atmosphere in concentrations that may be injurious to human beings, living creatures, plants, property, or the environment. Smoke, dust, fumes, gases, mist, vapour, and odorous substances are included.

## Section 19 — Declaration of Air Pollution Control Areas
The State Government, in consultation with the State Pollution Control Board, may declare any area an air pollution control area. Burning any material in such areas in a manner that causes or is likely to cause air pollution is prohibited without the Board's permission.

## Section 21 — Prohibition on Operating Industrial Plant Without Consent
No person shall establish or operate any industrial plant in an air pollution control area without the prior written consent of the State Pollution Control Board (KSPCB in Karnataka). Consent sets emission standards and operating conditions.

## Section 22 — Prohibition on Discharge Beyond Standards
No person in charge of any industrial plant shall discharge or permit discharge of any air pollutant in excess of the standards laid down by the State Board. This applies to particulate matter, SO2, NOx, CO, and other notified pollutants.

## Section 22A — Open Burning as Air Pollution Source
Open burning of waste — including municipal solid waste, agricultural residue, construction debris, plastic, rubber, or e-waste — on roads, open plots, or dump sites releases particulate matter and toxic gases and constitutes emission of air pollutants. Such burning in air pollution control areas is actionable under this Act.

## Section 31A — Enforcement Authority and Powers
The State Pollution Control Board (KSPCB) is the primary enforcement authority. It can issue directions to close, prohibit, or regulate operations. The CPCB provides national oversight and can issue directions that override State Board orders. Violations are cognisable by designated inspectors.

## Section 37 — Penalties
Failure to comply with the provisions or directions of the State Board attracts imprisonment of one and a half years to six years and a fine. Continuing violations attract additional fines for each day of default.
